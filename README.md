## Project Overview

This project focuses on the design, prototyping, and iterative refinement of a pop-up boat system using composite material principles and additive manufacturing techniques. The goal was to develop a lightweight, structurally efficient prototype capable of integrating a heat source and a pop-up boiler mechanism while maintaining manufacturability and functional performance.

---

## Design Objectives

The primary objectives of this project were to:

- Develop a compact and lightweight boat structure  
- Integrate a heat source support system  
- Incorporate a functional pop-up boiler mechanism  
- Optimize the design for 3D printing and material efficiency  
- Improve structural integrity through iterative design refinement  

---

## Initial Design and Prototype (Iteration 1)

![Boat Design]((https://raw.githubusercontent.com/farrishamid107/Pop-Pop-Boat_Composite_Materials/main/images/1st Iteration.png)the

The first prototype, fabricated in gray material, served as a baseline model to evaluate geometry, manufacturability, and structural feasibility.

### Key Characteristics
- Fully solid internal structure  
- Basic external hull geometry  
- Limited internal space for component integration  

### Identified Limitations
- Excessive material usage due to solid infill  
- Increased weight affecting efficiency and performance  
- Lack of internal cavities required for system integration  

---

## Engineering Modification

![Boat Design](2nd Iteration.png)

To address the limitations of the initial design, a shelling strategy was implemented. This involved removing excess internal material while preserving the external geometry.

### Outcomes of Modification
- Reduced overall weight  
- Improved material efficiency  
- Creation of internal volume for functional components  

---

## Intermediate Prototyping and Testing

During iterative testing, several manufacturing and design challenges were identified:

- Over-reliance on support structures for complex geometries  
- Print failures caused by unsupported overhangs  
- Structural inconsistencies due to uneven internal distribution  

These challenges informed further refinements and emphasized the importance of manufacturability-driven design.

---

## Final Design (Iteration 2)

![Boat Design](Final Iteration.png)

The final prototype, produced in purple material, represents an optimized version of the original concept, incorporating lessons learned from earlier iterations.

### Key Improvements
- Implementation of a lightweight shelled structure  
- Dedicated internal compartments for heat source integration  
- Functional space allocated for pop-up boiler mechanism  
- Improved geometry to reduce support material requirements  
- Enhanced structural integrity through reinforcement strategies  

### Design Outcomes
The final design achieves a balance between structural performance, manufacturability, and functional integration. It demonstrates improved print reliability and optimized material usage.

---

## Materials and Manufacturing Process

- **Manufacturing Method:** Fused Deposition Modeling (FDM)  
- **Material Type:** Composite-compatible thermoplastics (e.g., PLA-based blends or reinforced filaments)  
- **Design Approach:** Lightweight shell structure with targeted reinforcement  

---

## Engineering Insights

This project reinforced several key engineering principles:

- Iterative design is essential for optimizing performance and manufacturability  
- Shelling is an effective strategy for reducing weight without altering external geometry  
- Proper planning of support structures is critical in additive manufacturing  
- Functional integration should be considered early in the design process  

---

## Future Work

Future improvements to the design may include:

- Use of thermally resistant composite materials for improved heat management  
- Optimization of heat transfer efficiency within the boiler system  
- Application of fluid dynamics analysis to enhance buoyancy and motion  
- Exploration of multi-material additive manufacturing for added functionality  

---

## Conclusion

The pop-up boat project demonstrates the application of iterative engineering design, material optimization, and additive manufacturing techniques in developing a functional prototype. The evolution from a solid initial model to an optimized shelled structure highlights the importance of efficiency, structural planning, and early system integration in engineering design.
